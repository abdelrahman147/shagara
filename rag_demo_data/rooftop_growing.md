# shagara Rooftop Growing Guide
## Basil in Cairo
Basil performs best with 6 to 8 hours of bright light and airflow around the leaves. Keep the growing medium evenly moist, but let the top 2 cm dry before watering again. Harvest the top leaves weekly to encourage branching. Pinch flowers immediately to keep foliage tender and aromatic.

## Summer heat
During July and August, move containers away from reflective walls and use a 30 percent shade cloth between 11am and 3pm. Morning irrigation reduces evaporation and leaf stress. Mulching containers with 3 cm of clean straw lowers root zone temperatures by up to 6 degrees Celsius during Cairo heatwaves.

## Rooftop Tomatoes and Peppers
Tomatoes and sweet peppers require containers with a minimum depth of 35 cm and a volume of at least 25 litres per plant. Provide sturdy bamboo or wire cages anchored against Khamaseen desert winds. Apply organic calcium nitrate or crushed eggshell amendments into the potting mix to prevent blossom end rot in extreme daytime heat.

## Mint and Mediterranean Herbs
Mint species including spearmint and peppermint must always be grown in separate, dedicated containers to stop invasive runners from suffocating neighboring crops. Mint thrives with morning sun, afternoon dappled shade, and consistent soil dampness. Rosemary, thyme, and oregano prefer well-draining grit mixtures and can withstand full direct rooftop sun with moderate watering.

## Recommended Soil Mix and Rooftop Load
The optimal lightweight container substrate for Cairo rooftop farming consists of 50 percent coco coir or peat moss, 30 percent horticultural perlite, and 20 percent organic vermicompost. This blend maintains aeration, retains balanced moisture, and prevents excessive structural dead-weight on residential concrete rooftops. Avoid garden clay soil which compacts and adds hazardous weight.
